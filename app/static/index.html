What you actually get

Four things, done properly. No dashboards full of numbers nobody can trace.

Discovery across 9 real sources

Papers and datasets from OpenAlex, Crossref, Semantic Scholar, Europe PMC, Zenodo, DataCite, Hugging Face, UCI and Data.gov — each queried with several rewritten query variants, every page logged in a search ledger.

Verification, not vibes

Every record gets its DOI resolved against Crossref or DataCite, its links probed, its licence and access terms read. Checks are stored with their method and timestamp, so you can see exactly what was confirmed and what was not.

Evidence with a locator

Findings are extracted verbatim from text that was actually retrieved — never paraphrased into a claim. Every passage carries its locator, its origin and a link back to the source it came from.

An honest empty result

If the configured scope was explored and nothing suitable and verifiable turned up, you get one sentence. No filler, no plausible-sounding invention, and a failed source is never counted as proof that data doesn't exist.

What happens in the background

The full path from your question to a verified answer. Every stage writes to an audit trail you can read in the app.

  1. 1

    Your question is parsed

    Plain language becomes a structured specification: topic, objective, domain, intent (datasets / literature / both), keywords, data types, modalities, geography, time range and access requirements. Assumptions it had to make are listed, not hidden.

    parser.py
  2. 2

    A query plan is built

    The question is rewritten into several query variants per source, each tagged with where it came from. Adapters that don't fit the intent are excluded — with a stated reason.

    planning.py
  3. 3

    Official public APIs are contacted

    Each adapter handles its own pagination, retries, back-off and rate limits. Every page requested, every empty page and every failure is written to the search ledger — a failed source is recorded as a failure, never as "nothing exists".

    sources/*.py · http_client.py
  4. 4

    Everything is normalised

    Nine different response shapes collapse into one NormalizedRecord: identifier, DOI, title, abstract, authors, year, venue, publisher, licence, access, citation count, concepts, dataset fields and the raw payload.

    sources/base.py
  5. 5

    Duplicates are merged deterministically

    Same DOI → same record. Otherwise title + year, then a token-overlap check at 0.87. Merges are recorded as duplicate_of so the merge is reversible and countable, not a silent delete.

    services/dedupe.py
  6. 6

    Identity and links are verified

    DOIs are resolved through the registrars, links are probed, and licence / access terms are compared. The outcome is stored per check — verified, partially verified, conflict, inconclusive, inaccessible or failed.

    services/verification.py
  7. 7

    Sufficiency is judged

    The pipeline decides whether the scope it actually reached is enough to answer: sufficient, unverified candidates, insufficient evidence, or search incomplete. Only the last two are ever allowed to end in the "no dataset" reply.

    services/evidence.py
  8. 8

    Evidence is extracted, verbatim

    Passages are lifted from retrieved text with a locator and an origin marker. Metadata copied from a single source is labelled as provenance — explicitly not independent confirmation.

    services/evidence.py
  9. 9

    Everything is stored and reproducible

    Projects, runs, the ledger, records, verifications, downloads with SHA-256, gap candidates and manuscripts all land in local SQLite. Reopen any project later and the reasoning is still there.

    db.py · services/store.py
  10. 10

    You get the answer, or the truth

    Results appear with their verification status and evidence attached. If nothing survives, you get the honest line below — never a fabricated substitute.

    api/routes.py

The one rule that shapes everything

ResearchForge never fabricates data, sources, citations, dataset counts, findings, experiments or conclusions. When sufficient evidence cannot be found or verified, the platform says exactly this, verbatim:

There is insufficient data worldwide. No dataset.

  • It does not invent datasets, papers, citations, counts, results, significance or novelty.
  • It does not treat metadata copied from one source as independent confirmation.
  • It does not bypass logins, paywalls or licence restrictions.
  • It does not claim internet-wide coverage — results are scoped to the adapters that actually answered.
  • It does not write a results or conclusion section before experiments exist.

Sources, and how they're credited

Only official public endpoints are used. The live state of each adapter — availability, families of data it covers, and whether a key is configured — is shown inside the app, never guessed here.
